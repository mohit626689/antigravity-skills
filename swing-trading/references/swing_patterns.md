# High-Probability Swing Trading Chart Patterns

Swing trading seeks to capture short-term directional price moves (typically 3 to 15 trading days). The best setups combine **clear structural chart patterns**, **multi-timeframe moving average support**, and **institutional volume footprints**.

---

## 1. The Bull Flag (Momentum Continuation)

A classic setup after a sharp upward impulse.

```
       [Pole: Sharp Volume Surge]
                  /\
                 /  \   [Flag: Tight, Declining Volume Channel]
                /    \      \
               /      \  /\  \
              /        \/  \  \  <-- Buy Trigger on Breakout
             /              \  \
            /                \/
           /
```

### Key Criteria:
- **Flagpole**: 10%–25% advance over 3–8 days with expanding volume.
- **Flag**: Orderly, parallel downward or horizontal consolidation retracing no more than 38.2% to 50% of the pole.
- **Volume**: Must dry up noticeably during the flag formation.
- **Entry Trigger**: Daily close or 1-hour candle break above the upper boundary trendline.
- **Stop Loss**: 1 ATR below the lowest point of the flag channel.
- **Target**: Height of flagpole added to breakout point (1:2 to 1:3 R:R minimum).

## 2. The 20 EMA Pullback (Dip Buying in Strong Trends)

In a sustained bull trend, the 20-day Exponential Moving Average acts as dynamic support.

```
 Price:        /\            /\            /\ (Target)
              /  \          /  \          /
             /    \  <--   /    \  <--   /
            /      \      /      \      /
 20 EMA:   -------------------------------------
          (Bounce #1)    (Bounce #2 - Buy Trigger)
```

### Key Criteria:
- **Trend Filter**: Stock is trading above rising 50 SMA and 200 SMA.
- **Pullback Duration**: 2 to 4 consecutive red/narrow candles drifting back toward the 20 EMA.
- **Volume Signature**: Volume shrinks on down days (no institutional selling).
- **Entry Trigger**: Price touches or slightly dips into 20 EMA and prints a reversal candle (Hammer, Bullish Engulfing, or Inside Bar break).
- **Stop Loss**: Just below the swing low created at the 20 EMA test.
- **Target**: Prior swing high (Target 1: take 50% off, move stop to breakeven); Target 2: Trailing 20 EMA.

## 3. Cup and Handle (Base Breakout)

A foundational accumulation pattern developed over 3 to 7 weeks.

```
   Resistance Rim Line ----------------------------+ (Breakout Trigger)
                      \                           / \
                       \                         /   \ (Handle)
                        \                       /     \
                         \                     /
                          \___________________/
                              (Rounded Base)
```

### Key Criteria:
- **Left Side**: Gradual rounded decline, not a sharp V-shape.
- **Right Side**: Recovery toward the prior pivot high.
- **Handle**: Shallow downward drift (less than 12% deep) lasting 3 to 10 days on low volume.
- **Entry Trigger**: Price crosses the resistance rim on volume $> 1.5\times$ 20-day average.
- **Stop Loss**: Below the low of the handle.
- **Target**: Depth of the cup added to the breakout point.

## 4. Ascending Triangle (Aggressive Accumulation)

Indicates that buyers are willing to absorb supply at increasingly higher prices.

```
 Flat Resistance ----------------------------------+ (Breakout Trigger)
                /          /          /          /
               /          /          /          /
              /          /          /  Rising Lows
             /          /          /
            /          /
```

### Key Criteria:
- **Ceiling**: A flat horizontal resistance zone tested at least 2–3 times.
- **Floor**: Rising trendline forming distinct higher lows.
- **Compression**: Price action narrows toward the apex of the triangle.
- **Entry Trigger**: Clean daily breakout above horizontal resistance with high delivery/volume.
- **Stop Loss**: Below the most recent higher swing low.
- **Target**: Vertical height of the triangle projected upward.

---

## 5. Double Bottom with RSI Bullish Divergence (Reversal Setup)

Used to catch major reversals when an extended pullback reaches major historical support.

```
 Price:    \          /          \          / (Neckline Breakout)
            \  Low 1 /            \  Low 2 /
             \______/              \______/
              (Support Level)

 RSI:         \                   /
               \  Low 1          /  Low 2 (Higher Low = Bullish Divergence!)
                \______/        /______/
```

### Key Criteria:
- **Price**: Forms two distinct swing lows at approximately the same level.
- **RSI Divergence**: Price Low 2 is equal to or slightly lower than Low 1, while RSI Low 2 is noticeably **higher** than RSI Low 1.
- **Entry Trigger**: Breakout above the intermediate swing high (neckline) on expanding volume.
- **Stop Loss**: Below Low 2.
- **Target**: Neckline to trough height projected upward.
